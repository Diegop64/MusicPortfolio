Original music for indie games

I compose soundtracks for indie games in development: a main theme and the music for each map, level, battle or menu, delivered as files ready to integrate into the game. Commissions start at €50 and include an exclusive licence. I can also handle the sound design (sound effects, foley and the rest of the game's sounds).

Who this is for

Studios and solo developers with a commercial game in development, for Steam, consoles or any other store, who want music written for their game instead of library tracks. Most of my work so far has been for indie games, and one studio, Betaking Studios, has commissioned the music for three of its games.

I work remotely from Murcia, Spain, and accept commissions from any country. If your game is still a prototype you can write anyway: the quote gets more precise as the screens and levels that need music are defined.

Services and pricing

Commissions from €50. The final price depends on the minutes of music, the number of tracks and whether sound effects are included.

Every commission gets a written quote before work starts, listing the tracks and what each one is for, the licence, the delivery date and the revision rounds. These three options describe the usual scope; a project can also sit between two of them.

  • Small project

    From €50

    One or a few tracks: a title screen, a trailer, a cutscene or a few levels. Delivered ready for the game, with looped versions where a track needs them.

  • Soundtrack with several tracks

    Quoted by minutes and tracks

    A main theme plus the music for each map, world, battle or menu. Duelite, for example, has 11 tracks: the main theme, three for each of its three maps (map screen, battle and boss) and one for the shop.

  • Full soundtrack with sound design

    Quoted on scope

    Everything in the previous option plus the game's complete sound design: sound effects (FX), foley and the rest of the sounds the game needs.

If you are still working out a budget, the pricing guide explains what makes a soundtrack cost more or less, with the Duelite commission as an example and the market ranges published by other sources.

What you receive

The exact list goes in the quote. A game commission usually includes:

  • A main theme

    The piece that identifies the game, typically for the title screen and trailers. All three Betaking Studios games have one.

  • Music for each screen or state

    One track for each situation the player goes through (map, battle, boss, shop, world), written for what happens on screen.

  • Game-ready delivery

    Files prepared to integrate into the game, including looped versions when the track needs them. File formats are agreed in the quote.

  • Sound design, if you need it

    Sound effects (FX), foley and the rest of the game's sounds, quoted together with the music.

Deadlines and revisions

The deadline and the number of revision rounds are set in the quote, according to the scope. Tell me your key dates in the first message (a demo, a festival, Steam Next Fest, the launch) and I'll plan the deliveries around them.

You review each track before it's considered finished. On Duelite, as the studio mentions in its review, there were a couple of adjustments along the way to make the music fit the atmosphere of the game.

Rights and licence

Exclusive licence: the music for your project is only for your project. You can use it commercially in your game and I won't license it to anyone else.

I reserve the right to show it in my portfolio, on my website and on social media, from the game's release.

The details (uses, territory, duration, releasing the soundtrack on streaming platforms) are set in writing in each quote. This is a summary of how I work, not legal advice.

When to get in touch and what to send

You can write at any stage of development; the more defined the game, the more precise the quote. These three things are enough to start:

  • The screens or situations that need music

    For example: title screen, three maps, battles, boss fights and a shop. The quote is calculated from that list.

  • References

    Games, soundtracks or tracks with the tone you're after, and what you like about each one. Some ideas are hard to put into words; references help with that.

  • A build or video, and your dates

    A playable build, gameplay footage or screenshots, plus the date you need the music by. The form below has a field for the link.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the music for a game cost?

Commissions start at €50 for a small project. The final price depends on the minutes of music, the number of tracks and whether sound effects are included. I send a written quote for each project, and the pricing guide explains the criteria in detail.

What kind of music do you compose?

It depends on the game: orchestral and western (Duelite), retro and chiptune (Rabbit Trail), and other styles in my personal pieces. My favourite is orchestral, in the vein of Game of Thrones.

What is the process, and how do revisions work?

We start with a conversation about the game so I understand what you need. Then I send a written quote with the tracks, the licence, the deadline and the revision rounds. I compose, you review each track, and I adjust it within those rounds until it fits the game.

Do you also make the sound effects?

Yes. I offer complete sound design for games: sound effects (FX), foley and the rest of the game's sounds. If you need it, it goes in the same quote as the music.

Who owns the music once it's delivered?

You receive an exclusive licence for commercial use. I keep the right to show the music in my portfolio from the game's release. Uses, territory and duration are set in writing in the quote.
