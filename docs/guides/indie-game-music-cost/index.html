How much does music for an indie game cost?

My commissions start at €50 for a small project. For a full soundtrack, the price depends mainly on three things: how many minutes of music the game needs, how many tracks they are split into, and whether sound design is included; the licence and the deadline also play a part. Below I explain each factor, how a real commission (Duelite) is scoped, and the market ranges published by other sources.

By Diego Olmos

What makes the price go up or down

These are the criteria I use to prepare a quote:

  • Minutes of music

    The main measure. A one-minute menu loop is not the same amount of work as a three-minute boss theme.

  • Number of tracks and what each one does

    Each screen or state with its own music (map, battle, boss, shop, world) is a separate track to compose, and some need a looped version so they play without a cut.

  • Sound design

    Sound effects (FX), foley and the rest of the game's sounds are a separate job from the music. Including them raises the price.

  • Licence

    My quotes include an exclusive licence for commercial use. Other uses, such as releasing the soundtrack on streaming platforms, are agreed in the quote.

  • Deadline and revisions

    Both the deadline and the number of revision rounds are set in the quote. A short deadline means concentrating the work; if that affects the price, the quote says so.

Example: the scope of the Duelite soundtrack

Duelite is a western roguelite card game by Betaking Studios, released on Steam in November 2024. Its soundtrack has 11 tracks: a main theme; a map, a battle and a boss track for each of its three maps; and a shop track shared by all three maps.

For a quote, that list is the starting point. Changing the scope changes the number of tracks and therefore the price: two maps instead of three would remove three tracks, and a separate shop theme for each map would add two.

In its review the studio mentions "a couple of adjustments along the way". Revisions are part of the job, and the quote states how many rounds are included.

How I charge

I quote each commission in writing before starting. The quote is calculated from the minutes of music, the number of tracks and whether sound design is included, and it states the licence, the deadline and the revision rounds.

The minimum is €50, for a small project such as a title screen theme or a few short tracks. That figure is where the smallest commission starts, not a price per minute: a soundtrack with several tracks, or one with sound design, is quoted on its scope.

What other sources publish

These figures are not my prices. They are what two published sources say about the market, in US dollars per minute of music:

  • Game Developer, February 2023

    Winston Nguyen's article on commissioning music for a game gives $30–100 per minute for hobbyists, $200–400 for full-time indie composers and upwards of $1,000 for studio rates.

  • Ninichi, November 2018

    The game composer Ninichi gives a range of $50 to $2,500 per minute across the industry and $200–1,000 for experienced indie composers, and says budgets of $1,000–10,000 are common for indie games commissioning a custom soundtrack.

How the licence affects the price

The type of licence changes what you are paying for. Foxi Music's 2026 guide for game developers explains that with a non-exclusive licence other developers can license the same track, while with an exclusive licence only you can use it, and the price reflects that.

In my commissions the music is written for your game and the licence is exclusive: the music for your project is only for your project. I reserve the right to show it in my portfolio from the release. The details are set in writing in the quote.
